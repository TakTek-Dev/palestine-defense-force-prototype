Identity book · 27 October 2026

A legal-defense desk needs a mark that says what defense is: keeping a person in the record. This book sets out the mark, the grammar it gives the site, and how to use both.

01 · The idea

In a court record, the square bracket is the one place where someone other than the speaker may write: [emphasis added], [sic], [the witness pointed at the door]. It is the mark of the editor and of counsel. It inserts, clarifies, objects.

Our mark is a bracket holding a point. The point is a person. The bracket is the defense reaching over them and keeping them in the record. Nothing else in the identity is in colour; the point is printed in stamp violet, the ink of the rubber stamp on every affidavit, every permit and every receipt a military court hands a family.

The idea carries into the site as a grammar: the bracket a person wears tells you the process the law allows them.

02 · The mark

Built on a 64-unit grid. Stem and arms share one weight (8.6). Outer corners ease at 8, inner corners at 2.4, the arm ends at 3: soft, never knife-cut, and still unmistakably a bracket. The arms end at 47, past the point’s centre at 37.5: they reach over it. At icon sizes the point grows to 9.4 so it can never read as the middle bar of an E.

03 · Lockups

In the stacked lockup the bracket grows to span both lines and holds the name itself; its stem matches the stem of the letters (Mona Sans, width 112, weight 800). The point moves into the name: it is the dot of the i in Palestine, in stamp violet. Use the one-line wordmark only where height is short.

04 · The bracket grammar
  • SentencedTried and sentenced. The record is closed.
  • RemandHeld for interrogation or trial. The record is open.
  • Administrative detentionHeld without charge on a secret file, by renewable order.
  • “Unlawful combatant”Held outside the courts under the 2002 law, mostly from Gaza.
  • ReleasedOut of custody.
  • Died in custodyHeld by Israel when they died.

The same five shapes label people on case files, group the points on the docket and filter lists. The shape carries the meaning, so the colour never has to: they read in black and white, and on a screen reader each one is named.

The docket, testimony, the footer.

Stamp on night#9A90FF

The point and the count on night.

One colour, and it belongs to people. Stamp on paper passes 7:1; stamp on night is lifted to #9A90FF to pass 6:1. Status is never told by colour alone.

07 · The docket

The front page opens on the docket: every person held, one point each, grouped by the law that holds them, each group in its bracket. A scrubber walks back month by month to before October 2023, so the reader watches the groups without a bracket grow.

08 · Motion

Into the record: the bracket stands up, the name is entered line by line, and last the point drops into the i. Once a session, 1.6 seconds, skipped for reduced motion. Elsewhere, motion is small and has a reason: brackets close around a tab on hover, the case clocks count today, the docket fills once when it is first seen.

09 · Photographs

Photographs show the place or institution in the story, with their real date, in their own colour, resized only. No filters, no duotones. Where no honest photograph exists, the page prints the status mark on a quiet plate instead. We do not show the faces of children in custody.

10 · Voice

Plain and exact, in the order a lawyer would want it: who, held where, under what law, since when, what happens next. Say “held without charge”, not “detained”. Name the law. Give the date. Put the source on the same page. Use the people’s own words in Petrona italic and attribute them.
